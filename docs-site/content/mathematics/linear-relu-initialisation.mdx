This page covers three building blocks from `ops.py`: the linear layer, ReLU and Xavier uniform. It also covers how `init_params` uses them. In the backward sections, `dM` always means the gradient of the scalar loss with respect to `M`.

## Helper or inline?

The linear layer is written in two ways, so reading only `ops.py` gives the wrong picture.

- **Forward: always inline.** `transformer.py` does not import `linear_forward`, and only `test_linear_worked_example` calls it. The model writes `X @ W + b` directly at <CodeLine id="linear.q" /> (query), <CodeLine id="linear.k" /> (key), <CodeLine id="linear.v" /> (value), <CodeLine id="linear.out" /> (attention output), <CodeLine id="linear.ff1" /> and <CodeLine id="linear.ff2" /> (feed-forward), and <CodeLine id="linear.head" /> (head).
- **Backward: both.** `linear_backward` is called at <CodeLine id="linear.back-ff2" /> and <CodeLine id="linear.back-ff1" /> (feed-forward), and <CodeLine id="linear.back-head" /> (head). `attention_backward` does not call it. It writes the same three formulas in place at <CodeLine id="linear.back-out" /> (attention output) and <CodeLine id="linear.back-qkv" /> (query, key and value).

**How this reaches a parameter update.** The caller adds `dW` and `db` to `grads`, for example at <CodeLine id="update.head" /> and <CodeLine id="update.ff" />. `batch_loss_and_grads` averages `grads` over the batch (<CodeLine id="train.gradient" />). `clip_grad_norm` may scale it (<CodeLine id="train.clip" />), and `opt.step` uses it to change the parameter (<CodeLine id="train.adamw" />). `dX` is not stored. It goes to the previous layer as its `dY`. <PageLink page="training/how-the-model-learns">How the Model Learns</PageLink> follows the whole path.
