## Deriving the backward formula

<ConceptStep>What is it?</ConceptStep>

<ConceptCard concept="ops.layer_norm_derivation" split />

<ConceptStep>Why does the model need it?</ConceptStep>

<ConceptWhy concept="ops.layer_norm_derivation" />

<ConceptStep>Mathematics</ConceptStep>

Start from the loss and go back one step. `Y` depends on the normalised row through `gamma`.

<MathBlock id="ln.derive.chain-start" />

Next, work out how the normalised row depends on the input. It depends on `mu` and `s`, and `s` depends on the variance. Every entry of the row changes the mean by the same amount.

<MathBlock id="ln.derive.mean" />

The sum of `X - mu` over a row is 0, so the term from the mean drops out.

<MathBlock id="ln.derive.var" />

Epsilon is a constant, so it stays inside the power. That power of `var + epsilon` is `s` cubed, exactly.

<MathBlock id="ln.derive.s" />

Apply the product rule to `(X - mu) * s`. In the second term, `(X - mu) * s` appears twice, and each is a `Xhat`.

<MathBlock id="ln.derive.xhat" />

Multiply by `dXhat` and sum over the features of the row. Each `1/d` and its sum is one of the two means in the code, which gives the formula above and its code lines.

<MathBlock id="ln.derive.result" />

`gamma` and `beta` enter directly. Each is shared by every row, which is why their gradients are sums over rows.

## Parameters

Each block has two LayerNorms, and the model has one more at the end. Each has its own pair of vectors, and `init_params` sets `gamma` to ones and `beta` to zeros. So at the start of training, the output of each LayerNorm is exactly `Xhat`.
